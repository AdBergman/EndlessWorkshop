# Codex Spiritual Parent Review

Ticket: EW-CODEX-UI-ART-004
Date: 2026-06-24
Status: product vision; no implementation
Area: Codex creative direction

## Purpose

This review asks which Endless Legend 2 interface family should become the
emotional parent of the EWShop Codex.

The goal is not to copy a game screen or redesign the page. The goal is to
find the native EL2 emotional language that Codex should inherit.

## Framing

Quest Explorer works because it inherits the emotional job of quests: the
player is experiencing an unfolding chronicle.

Codex needs the same kind of native parent. "Imperial Atlas" is evocative, but
it is an external metaphor unless it is anchored to something Endless Legend 2
already does emotionally.

The question is not "Which screen looks like Codex?" The question is "Which
screen performs the same psychological job?"

## Emotional Analysis Of Major EL2 Screen Families

### Technology / Research

Emotional job:
The player is planning civilization change. They are turning uncertainty into a
path: what to unlock, when to unlock it, and what future options become
available.

Visual/psychological role:
Technology screens often feel like structured discovery. Nodes, eras, branches,
research duration, unlocks, and completion states create a mental model of the
world's systems.

What matters emotionally:
Research makes knowledge actionable. It is not only reference; it is a map of
possibility.

Codex relevance:
Very high. Codex is not a story route and not a city operation panel. It is a
knowledge system that helps the player understand relationships, mechanics,
and strategic consequences.

### Hero View

Emotional job:
The player is investing in a champion. They are reading identity, capacity,
growth, and specialization.

Visual/psychological role:
Hero screens combine portrait identity, stats, equipment, skill paths, and
future development. They make a single entity feel valuable and personal.

What matters emotionally:
Attachment and investment. The hero is not a record; the hero is a strategic
asset with a future.

Codex relevance:
Medium-high for detail pages. Hero View is excellent for entity dossiers, but
too personal and character-centered to parent the whole Codex.

### Diplomacy

Emotional job:
The player is negotiating power, trust, leverage, and cost.

Visual/psychological role:
Diplomacy screens put another power in the room. They emphasize relationship
state, treaty choices, locked agreements, portraits, and transactional
consequence.

What matters emotionally:
Tension and social strategy.

Codex relevance:
Low-medium. Diplomacy has strong presence, but it is relationship theater.
Codex should explain diplomacy; it should not feel like negotiation.

### Empire

Emotional job:
The player is governing. They are assessing the health, direction, output, and
identity of the empire as a whole.

Visual/psychological role:
Empire screens tend to aggregate systems: resources, policies, populations,
territory, and priorities.

What matters emotionally:
Stewardship and command.

Codex relevance:
Medium. Codex shares the breadth of empire management, but Codex is not about
making immediate governing decisions. It is prior knowledge, not command.

### Cities

Emotional job:
The player is operating and optimizing a specific settlement.

Visual/psychological role:
City screens focus on production, population, construction, districts, units,
and local yields.

What matters emotionally:
Conversion of place into output.

Codex relevance:
Medium for districts, improvements, populations, and constructibles. Too
operational to parent the whole Codex.

### Army / Combat

Emotional job:
The player is positioning force and resolving tactical risk.

Visual/psychological role:
Army and combat interfaces emphasize units, movement, attack, terrain,
formation, and immediate consequences.

What matters emotionally:
Readiness, pressure, and execution.

Codex relevance:
Low-medium. Useful for unit and ability rows, but too tactical and immediate
for the encyclopedia's broad role.

### Construction / Production

Emotional job:
The player is choosing what the empire becomes next through build priorities.

Visual/psychological role:
Construction screens turn resources into built reality: districts, units,
improvements, and infrastructure.

What matters emotionally:
Commitment and opportunity cost.

Codex relevance:
Medium. It offers strong planning language for Improvements and Districts, but
the full Codex covers more than build queues.

### Quest

Emotional job:
The player is experiencing story, choice, and consequence.

Visual/psychological role:
Quest screens carry progression, narrative, branch awareness, and emotional
stakes.

What matters emotionally:
Chronicle and discovery through events.

Codex relevance:
Low as a parent, because Quest Explorer already owns this language. Codex must
not become a second quest route.

### Encyclopedia / Help

Emotional job:
The player is looking up facts.

Visual/psychological role:
Help and encyclopedia systems tend to be direct, searchable, and taxonomic.

What matters emotionally:
Confidence and retrieval.

Codex relevance:
High functionally, but weak creatively. If Codex inherits only encyclopedia
language, it remains useful but forgettable.

### Victory

Emotional job:
The player is measuring ambition and end-state progress.

Visual/psychological role:
Victory screens frame long-term goals, requirements, thresholds, and race
conditions.

What matters emotionally:
Destiny, pressure, and strategic orientation.

Codex relevance:
Medium-low. Victory has strong directionality, but too narrow a scope.

### Faction Selection

Emotional job:
The player is choosing identity before play begins.

Visual/psychological role:
Faction selection introduces personality, strengths, fantasy, and playstyle.

What matters emotionally:
Invitation and self-definition.

Codex relevance:
Medium-high for the landing. It teaches how to make categories feel like
distinct worlds. But Codex is not only about choosing a civilization.

### Loading Screens

Emotional job:
The player is entering the world.

Visual/psychological role:
Loading screens can create myth, atmosphere, and expectation with broad visual
tone and sparse information.

What matters emotionally:
Arrival.

Codex relevance:
Medium. Useful for mood and first impression, but too passive to guide a
functional reference product.

### Strategic Overlays / Map Reading

Emotional job:
The player is interpreting the world layer: territory, resources, threat,
movement, and opportunity.

Visual/psychological role:
Overlays turn the game world into readable strategy. They are not purely
decorative; they are interpretive lenses.

What matters emotionally:
Surveying and orientation.

Codex relevance:
High conceptually. The Codex should feel like a lens over game knowledge. But
map overlays are tied to spatial play, while Codex must organize abstract
systems too.

## Top Five Candidate Parent Screens

### 1. Technology / Research

Strengths:
- closest emotional match to Codex as strategic knowledge;
- turns information into future-facing planning;
- naturally supports unlocks, relationships, categories, and dependencies;
- feels native to 4X play rather than generic archive design.

Weaknesses:
- can become too node/graph-driven if copied literally;
- risks overemphasizing progression when many Codex categories are reference
  sets rather than research trees.

What Codex should borrow:
- the feeling of structured discovery;
- research-table authority;
- future-facing planning energy;
- the sense that knowledge has relationships and consequences.

What Codex must not borrow:
- literal tech-tree layout;
- branch/path complexity for every category;
- node graph interaction as the default landing model.

### 2. Strategic Overlay / Map Reading

Strengths:
- aligns with the earlier Imperial Atlas idea;
- makes Codex feel like a survey lens over the world;
- reinforces 4X orientation and mastery.

Weaknesses:
- can become too geographic when Codex includes abstract rules;
- may encourage decorative map language that does not help lookup.

What Codex should borrow:
- the feeling of surveying a world layer;
- directional reading and purposeful depth;
- knowledge as an interpretive overlay.

What Codex must not borrow:
- literal territory/map UI;
- tactical map controls;
- decorative cartography that slows scanning.

### 3. Faction Selection

Strengths:
- emotionally memorable;
- teaches how to present identity, fantasy, and playstyle;
- useful for category cards as distinct destinations.

Weaknesses:
- too identity/choice focused for a reference system;
- could make the landing feel like a character select screen.

What Codex should borrow:
- strong category identity;
- immediate sense of faction/world personality;
- premium invitation into the setting.

What Codex must not borrow:
- hero/faction marketing poses;
- oversized spotlight treatment for every category;
- the sense that the player is choosing one path rather than exploring all.

### 4. Empire

Strengths:
- broad systems view;
- supports aggregation and cross-category thinking;
- feels strategic and managerial.

Weaknesses:
- risks dashboard energy, which is exactly what Codex has been trying to
  escape;
- too close to administration rather than discovery.

What Codex should borrow:
- system-level clarity;
- confidence that the world is governable and legible.

What Codex must not borrow:
- KPI/dashboard presentation;
- operational widgets;
- production/accounting mood.

### 5. Hero View

Strengths:
- excellent for detail pages and dossiers;
- makes individual entries feel valuable;
- supports stats, skills, identity, and progression.

Weaknesses:
- not broad enough for the landing;
- too character-centered and investment-focused.

What Codex should borrow:
- entity dignity on detail pages;
- compact but expressive stat/identity presentation;
- the idea that some records deserve profile treatment.

What Codex must not borrow:
- portrait-first hierarchy for generic entries;
- skill-tree/planner semantics where not warranted;
- personal progression framing for all categories.

## Winning Direction

The strongest spiritual parent is **Technology / Research**, with **Strategic
Overlay / Map Reading** as its atmospheric sibling.

This changes the earlier conclusion.

"Imperial Atlas" remains useful, but it is not the root. The root should be:

**Research Atlas**

Codex should feel like a research table for mastering Saiadha: a place where
the player studies the world, understands systems, and prepares better
strategic decisions.

This is stronger than pure Imperial Atlas because it is native to Endless
Legend 2's own emotional language. Technology/Research already teaches players
that knowledge is structured, consequential, and future-facing. Strategic
overlays add the sense of world survey and interpretation. Together, they give
Codex a specific identity:

```text
Not a wiki.
Not a dashboard.
Not a quest chronicle.
A research atlas for strategy mastery.
```

## Have We Been Solving The Wrong Problem?

Partly, yes.

The problem was not only that Codex lacked atmosphere. The deeper problem was
that the metaphor was floating outside the game. "Imperial Atlas" sounded
premium, but it did not fully answer why this should be the Codex of Endless
Legend 2 specifically.

The stronger move is to inherit from the game's own planning language:
Technology/Research. That gives Codex a native emotional job:

The player is not merely browsing facts. The player is researching the world in
order to master it.

## Concrete Design Principles Codex Should Inherit

1. **Structured discovery.**
   The page should imply that knowledge has relationships, unlocks, and
   consequences, even when the landing remains alphabetical.

2. **Research-table seriousness.**
   Search, metadata, and categories should feel like study instruments, not
   generic web controls.

3. **World-survey atmosphere.**
   Depth and light should suggest the player is reading a world layer, not
   opening a documentation page.

4. **Future-facing utility.**
   Codex should help players make better decisions later: what to build, what
   to recruit, what to research, what to counter, what to pursue.

5. **Category identity without spectacle.**
   Each category should feel like a distinct field of study, not a dashboard
   tile.

6. **Exactness as trust.**
   The Codex's precision should feel like scholarly authority, not database
   exposure.

## Design Principles Codex Should Reject

1. **Quest chronicle language.**
   Quest Explorer owns narrative progression. Codex should not inherit quest
   pacing, branch rhythm, or story reveal.

2. **Dashboard governance.**
   Empire-style aggregation can inform clarity, but Codex must not become KPI
   panels.

3. **Literal tech-tree copying.**
   Research is the emotional parent, not a layout mandate.

4. **Hero-profile treatment everywhere.**
   Dossiers work for heroes, units, factions, and some artifacts. They should
   not become the universal Codex grammar.

5. **Museum passivity.**
   Codex should be beautiful, but not merely contemplative. It must remain a
   strategy tool.

## Implications For Landing Page

The landing should feel like entering a research atlas.

It should:
- make search feel like consultation;
- make metadata feel like edition/source authority;
- make categories feel like fields of study;
- imply relationships and mastery without adding diagrams or complexity;
- create arrival through composed light, rhythm, and publication marks.

The landing should not:
- feel like a web index;
- feel like an admin dashboard;
- prioritize title scale over field composition;
- make every category an equal flat card with no sense of knowledge structure.

## Implications For Category Pages

Category pages should feel like focused research sections.

They should:
- preserve fast scanning;
- make filters/rails feel like research lenses;
- keep rows comparative and useful;
- show exact relationships where they improve planning;
- maintain a sense of source-truth discipline.

They should not:
- become mini dashboards;
- over-render every relationship equally;
- turn sparse categories into noisy mechanics dumps;
- copy Quest Explorer's progression language.

## Implications For Detail Pages

Detail pages should feel like research plates or dossiers depending on the
entry type.

They should:
- prioritize what makes the entry strategically meaningful;
- distinguish identity, mechanics, unlocks, and relationships;
- use exact links as scholarly references;
- keep player value above metadata completeness.

They should not:
- expose source data just because it exists;
- imply progression/planner semantics where source data is incomplete;
- make all entries feel equally important.

## Implications For Future Planners

Future planner-style features should inherit more directly from Technology,
Hero View, City, and Construction screens depending on domain.

Examples:
- technology planning may borrow Research more literally;
- hero planning may borrow Hero View more strongly;
- constructible planning may borrow City/Construction;
- faction strategy may borrow Faction Selection plus Empire.

Codex itself should remain the research atlas that connects these domains, not
the place where every planner is embedded.

## Final Recommendation

Reframe the Codex creative direction from **Imperial Atlas** to
**Research Atlas**.

Imperial Atlas solved the need for premium atmosphere. Research Atlas solves
the deeper product identity: Codex is the player's structured study surface for
understanding and mastering Endless Legend 2.

## One-Sentence Design Philosophy

Codex should feel like the research atlas of Saiadha: a precise, atmospheric
study surface where every system becomes legible enough to master.
